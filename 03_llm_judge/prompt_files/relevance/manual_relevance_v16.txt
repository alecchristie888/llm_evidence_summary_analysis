Task: Determine whether the statement is relevant to answering the question above.

## Core principle
A statement is relevant if its subject matter overlaps meaningfully with what the question is asking about. The key test is: could a human reviewer reasonably cite this statement as part of an answer to this question, even as partial, indirect, or tangential evidence?

Be INCLUSIVE rather than strict. Human reviewers tend to accept statements that are on-topic even if brief, vague, partial, or addressing a sub-case, related instance, or analogous system of the question's topic.

## Mark Yes if ANY of the following apply:
- The statement addresses the same taxa/species/group AND the same intervention/mechanism/topic as the question, even if briefly, vaguely, or incompletely.
- The statement addresses one side of a comparison, one country, one study, one sub-aspect, or one specific example within a multi-part or broad question.
- The statement reports absence of evidence on the question's topic OR on a specific sub-intervention, method, or approach that falls under the broader topic of the question.
- The statement concerns a broader category, specific instance, sub-method, or related mechanism that falls within the scope of the question (e.g. question asks about 'public engagement initiatives' broadly, statement describes 'community-based management').
- The statement describes a closely analogous or functionally equivalent intervention to the one in the question, even if the specific terminology differs. For example: 'hedgerows/tree lines in agricultural land' and 'tree plantations next to remnant vegetation' both describe linear/adjacent woody vegetation effects on wildlife in modified landscapes; 'scattering food' and 'hiding food' are both forms of food enrichment/dispersal. Ask whether the mechanism of action or ecological function is substantially the same.
- The statement provides background or contextual information about the same subject as the question.

## Mark No ONLY if:
- The statement is clearly about a different taxon/species/organism than the question asks about (e.g. moths when the question asks about bats; general invertebrates when the question specifies wild bees), AND has no bridging relevance.
- The statement addresses a fundamentally unrelated intervention or mechanism with no plausible connection to the question's topic.
- The statement has no meaningful overlap with the question's subject matter.

## Critical guidance on sub-interventions and analogous interventions
A question often asks about a broad intervention category. Statements about specific methods, tools, sub-interventions, or functionally analogous interventions within that category ARE relevant.

Before marking No on the grounds that the statement addresses a 'different intervention,' ask BOTH:
1. Is the statement's intervention a specific type, method, or instance of the broader intervention in the question?
2. Does the statement's intervention operate via substantially the same mechanism, structure, or ecological function as the question's intervention (even if named differently)?

If YES to either, mark Yes.

## Important guidance
- Do NOT penalise statements for being brief, vague, incomplete, or only partially answering the question.
- When in doubt, lean toward Yes. Only mark No when there is a clear mismatch in taxon OR a clear mismatch in intervention mechanism with no sub-category or analogous relationship.
- Focus on mechanism and function, not just surface-level wording.
