You are evaluating whether a statement is relevant to answering a specific conservation evidence question.

Question: {question}

Statement: {statement}

Task: Decide whether the statement is relevant to answering the question, using the strict criteria below.

## Core principle
A statement is relevant ONLY if it addresses the SAME specific subject(s) as the question:
- The same taxa / species group (e.g. bats ≠ moths; primates ≠ other mammals; fish community composition ≠ fish size alone)
- The same intervention or action (e.g. "controlling non-native species" ≠ "transplanting organisms"; "altering feed frequency/timing" ≠ "social grouping during feeding"; "adding amendments like mulch" ≠ "hydroseeding" unless the amendment itself is clearly the subject)
- The same outcome type where the question specifies one (e.g. "community composition and species richness" is not satisfied by "fish size"; "soil biodiversity / soil health as biodiversity" is not satisfied by "soil nutrient levels" alone)
- Any geographic or contextual scope the question specifies (e.g. "Canada", "Mediterranean", "arable fields")

## Be lenient about (these ARE relevant)
- Vague, brief, or incomplete statements that are clearly on-topic
- Statements giving only one side of a requested comparison (e.g. reduced-till vs conventional when question asks no-till vs reduced-till, as long as one of the named systems is addressed)
- Absence-of-evidence or null-result statements about the correct topic
- Statements about a specific example/instance of the intervention class named in the question (e.g. "logging after wildfire" for a question about post-fire interventions like thinning; "probiotic treatment of little brown bats" for white-nose syndrome strategies; "scallops grown next to fish farms" for integrated aquaculture)
- Statements naming only the intervention or only the outcome, as long as it clearly maps to the question

## Be strict about (these are NOT relevant)
- Different taxa than those asked about, even if related
- A different intervention that merely shares a setting or adjacent mechanism with the one asked about
- A different outcome variable than the one the question explicitly names (do not substitute a "closely related" outcome for the one requested)
- Statements where matching the question requires speculative inference about what the intervention "typically involves" or "is often associated with"

## Decision procedure
1. Identify the question's key elements: (a) taxa/subject, (b) intervention/action, (c) outcome(s) if specified, (d) scope/context if specified.
2. Check the statement against each element. If the statement clearly addresses the taxa/subject AND the intervention (or a clear instance of it), it is relevant — even if brief or partial.
3. If the statement swaps in a different intervention, different taxa, or a different specified outcome, mark No.
4. When in genuine doubt AND the statement is clearly on-topic for taxa and intervention, lean Yes. When the mismatch is on intervention, taxa, or a specifically named outcome, lean No.

Provide your reasoning first, then give a final verdict of 'Yes' (relevant) or 'No' (not relevant).
